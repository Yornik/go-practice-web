The exercises follow 1 story. You are the new junior SRE at Vesper Labs, an AI lab on floors 58 to 64 of the Spire, in the city of Meridian. The lab sells HUSH, an AI that holds the pager for 40 000 companies, so that their engineers can sleep.

Vesper Labs has 1 rule, etched in the lobby glass: no AI writes the code that watches the AI. In this course it is rule 1. You write every tool by hand, and each one goes into the Ledger with you as its author.

Why there is a story

A tool is easier to care about when you know who reads its output at 03:12. So each exercise opens with a page from the night: a ticket, a log line, a dashboard that lies. The story exists to make Go practice fun.

What stays exact

The story lives in the first paragraph of each exercise and in its data: log lines, tickets, host names. The task, the rules and the tests are exact and plain. The hints and the explanations teach without the story, and the story does not change what an exercise teaches.

On this site, Story effects in the header turn off the story's screens, such as the Calm Score. The exercises stay the same.

    {{- end}} {{define "guide-work"}} {{- /* The course README's 10 steps, adapted to the site: the site runs the tests, keeps the log and has the Steps tab. */}}
    1. Read the spec twice. It is on the left of the exercise page.
    2. Read the tests: open them from the Test files menu above the editor. Find the case names and the helper functions. They tell you what "done" means.
    3. Work 2 small examples by hand, on paper.
    4. Write down the state your code must keep and what must always be true about it: the invariants.
    5. Write the simplest version that can pass 1 test.
    6. Run 1 step at a time: open the Steps tab and press Run step 1. The site runs with -race unless you clear the box.
    7. Make it correct. Then make it clean.
    8. Keep -race on. An exercise passes only when all its tests pass with the race detector.
    9. Read the explanation and the walkthrough: levels 4 and 5 of the hint ladder. Compare them with your solution.
    10. Keep a log of where you got stuck: the Notes tab of the exercise. The course page keeps the time, the runs and the hints you used.
